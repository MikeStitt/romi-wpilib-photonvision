Would there be pro's to switching to the architecture that XRP uses?Yes, migrating the Romi's underlying communication logic to mirror the XRP architecture introduces several significant benefits. Doing so shifts your project from a heavy, custom WebSocket service dependency to a lightweight, firmware-driven implementation.If you are updating Romi support to the latest WPILib, standardizing its architecture to match the XRP model offers distinct advantages and challenges:The Pros of Switching to the XRP ArchitectureEliminating the Raspberry Pi Stack: The Romi architecture relies on a Raspberry Pi running a complex Linux OS stack, Node.js, and wpilib-ws-robot packages just to bridge I2C data. The XRP architecture bypasses heavy middleware entirely. It boots directly into lightweight C++ firmware on a microcontroller (Raspberry Pi Pico W) to handle incoming network packets with minimal overhead.Drastically Faster Boot Times: A Linux-based Romi image takes 30 to 45 seconds to boot up and initialize its WebSocket server. An XRP-style firmware architecture boots instantly (under 2 seconds), making rapid power-cycling during testing much less frustrating.Cleaner Network Topology: The standard Romi setup forces you to maintain an active WebSocket connection client (HALSIMWS) over standard TCP. Adapting to the XRP model allows you to transition your simulation hooks to a more lightweight protocol layer (HALSIMXRP), which handles hardware communication seamlessly behind the scenes.Reduced Power Consumption & Component Cost: Operating a full Linux CPU just to parse PWM and Encoder pulses quickly drains AA batteries. A micro-controller architecture is vastly more power-efficient and cuts down on hardware costs since you do not need to purchase or source a secondary Raspberry Pi.The Cons & Trade-offs (Why You Might Hesitate)The "No Onboard Vision" Limitation: This is the most significant downside. Because a Raspberry Pi runs a full operating system, you can plug in a USB webcam and run PhotonVision or custom OpenCV scripts directly on the robot. If you downgrade the Romi's brain to an XRP-style microcontroller architecture, you lose the ability to process vision on-board due to severe memory and processing limitations.Custom Firmware Development: There is no official, out-of-the-box "XRP-style firmware" configured for Romi pinouts. To make this switch, you would have to write custom firmware for a microcontroller (like an ESP32 or Pico W) that can talk to the Romi's TI MSP430 coprocessor over I2C, while simultaneously mimicking the custom HALSIM packet structure that WPILib expects.The VerdictSwitch to the XRP Architecture if: You are primarily focused on standard drivetrain programming, basic autonomous paths, gyro tracking, and sensor logic, and you want a snappy, stable connection that boots instantly.Stick with the Traditional Romi Stack if: Your curriculum or team testing relies heavily on vision tracking, AprilTags, or streaming camera feeds to a dashboard.Quick questions if you have time:Will you use computer vision?Yes, AprilTags/CamerasNo, just encoders/gyroWhich hardware language do you prefer?Microcontroller C++Raspberry Pi Linux9 sitesGetting Started with XRP - FIRST Robotics Competition - WPILibOct 4, 2025 — The XRP, powered by Worcester Polytechnic Institute, is a small and inexpensive robot designed for learning about programming FRC ...WPILibReleases · wpilibsuite/xrp-wpilib-firmware - GitHubOct 17, 2025 — Difference between XRP and Romi capabilities.
